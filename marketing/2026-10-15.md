## Day 1 (Thu) — Reddit r/resumes

**Title:** 15 before/after resume bullet rewrites (the one formula behind all of them)

Most resume bullets describe the job, not the person doing it well. The fix is almost always the same shape:

**Action verb + what you did + measurable result**

A few examples:

- "Responsible for weekly reports" → "Automated 12 weekly KPI reports in Tableau, saving the analytics team 6 hours a week"
- "Managed social media accounts" → "Grew LinkedIn followers from 2K to 11K in 9 months with a three-posts-a-week calendar"
- "Answered support tickets" → "Resolved 60+ tickets a day with a 96% CSAT, highest on a team of nine"
- "Worked at a restaurant" → "Trained 6 new servers and ran 40-cover Friday shifts as the youngest shift lead"

Quick test: read only the first four words of each bullet. If it's "Responsible for", "Helped with" or "Worked on", rewrite it.

No number? Use scale (team size, volume, users), time saved, or a before/after state.

## Day 3 (Sat) — LinkedIn

Most people think an ATS "rejects" their resume.

It mostly doesn't. An applicant tracking system is a database. It parses your resume into fields, stores it, and lets recruiters search.

The real problem is quieter:
→ A two-column table can attach your dates to the wrong job
→ Skills inside an image header are invisible to the parser
→ If you're missing the 3 keywords the recruiter types into search, you never show up

You don't get rejected. You just never surface.

## Day 5 (Mon) — X thread

**1/** Most behavioral interview answers fail on proportion, not structure.
People spend 60 seconds on the backstory and 10 on what they did. Flip it. 🧵

**2/** STAR in a 2-minute answer:
Situation + Task: 20 sec · Action: 60–70 sec · Result: 20–30 sec, with a number

**3/** Say "I", not "we". They're hiring you, not your old team.
"We migrated the DB" → nothing.
"I wrote the plan, ran the dry runs, handled the 2am cutover" → hireable.

**4/** You don't need 40 answers. You need 6–8 stories:
pressure · failure · conflict · leading without authority · improving a process · ambiguity · proudest result

## Day 7 (Wed) — Reddit r/jobs or r/careerguidance

**Title:** Cover letters: a 150-word structure hiring managers actually finish

Most cover letters restate the resume in longer sentences. What works better:

1. **Opening (1–2 sentences):** the role + the single most relevant thing about you. Skip "I am writing to apply for".
2. **Proof (one short paragraph per top requirement, max 2):** one example each, with a result.
3. **Close (1–2 sentences):** suggest a next step. Stop.

Delete on sight: "passionate, results-driven team player", "I believe I'd be a great fit", "Please find attached my resume".

Test: if you could send the same letter to another company by swapping the name, it isn't specific enough.
