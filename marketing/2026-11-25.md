## Day 1 (Mon) — Reddit r/linkedin (or r/careerguidance)

**Title:** Your LinkedIn About section probably shows two lines. Here's how to make them count.

LinkedIn only shows the first couple of lines of your About section before "see more", and on a phone it's even less. For most visitors, those lines *are* your About section.

What I'd put there, and after it:

1. **Open with what you do, not who you are.** "I build backend systems that stay boring under load" beats "Results-driven professional with a passion for excellence". The second sentence could belong to anyone.
2. **Then 2–4 lines of proof.** Things you built, shipped, fixed or ran. If you don't have exact numbers, use scope ("for a team of twelve", "every quarter since 2023"). Don't invent figures; they turn into awkward interview questions.
3. **Put your keywords in sentences.** Recruiters search profile text. "I mostly work in SQL, dbt and Looker" is just as searchable as a 30-skill comma list and doesn't read like spam.
4. **End with a next step.** Which roles you're open to, or what people should message you about. Most profiles never say.
5. **First person.** "Jane is a seasoned professional" reads like a press release she wrote about herself.

150–300 words is plenty. LinkedIn allows 2,600 characters, but nobody reads that far.

I wrote this up with four example About sections (engineer, career changer, recent grad, plus the mistakes list): https://www.aurapal.org/blog/linkedin-about-section-examples

Happy to give feedback on anyone's opening two lines in the comments.

## Day 3 (Wed) — LinkedIn

Most LinkedIn About sections open like this:

"Results-driven professional with a passion for excellence and a proven track record of delivering value."

That sentence is in thousands of profiles. It tells the reader nothing, and it's the part they're most likely to see, because LinkedIn cuts the About section off after a couple of lines.

A structure that works better:

→ Line 1–2: what you actually do, for whom, or the problem you like solving
→ Next: 2–4 lines of concrete proof (scope is fine if you don't have numbers)
→ Then: the skills you want to be found for, inside real sentences
→ Last: what you want the reader to do

Example for a career changer:

"Former secondary school teacher, now working in data analysis. For six years I built the spreadsheets my maths department used to track student progress, and I found I liked the data work more than the marking."

No adjectives. Just the work, and the reason for the switch.

Full guide with more examples: https://www.aurapal.org/blog/linkedin-about-section-examples
